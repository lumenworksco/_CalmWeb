Our commitments to student wellbeing.

Technology for students should work for the student, not extract from them. These are the principles CalmCampus is built on today, what data it uses, and what we will measure in our KU Leuven pilot.

Principles

Six things we hold to.

Each of these describes the app as it is built now. If that changes, this page changes with it.

On your phone, by design

Check-in answers and health signals are stored on the device. The current app has no server to send them to.

  • Steps and sleep are read from Apple Health, on the phone
  • Check-in answers are saved on the phone
  • No account, name or email is needed to use the app

No cloud AI, no analytics

Any AI runs on the device. We do not use ad networks, trackers or analytics services.

  • Nothing you write is sent to an outside AI service
  • No advertising identifiers
  • No usage or crash analytics

Real data or none

Every signal shown is real, missing or unavailable. We never fill gaps with made-up values.

  • No fabricated scores or streaks
  • If your phone has no sleep data, we ask you instead
  • Missing stays missing

Light, and always skippable

The daily check-in takes about a minute. You can skip any question.

  • Three core questions: mood, energy and stress
  • Up to two short follow-ups, only when they help
  • No clinical questionnaires and no scored cut-offs

Wellbeing, not diagnosis

CalmCampus supports and spots early signs. It does not diagnose, treat or replace care.

  • Plain, calm wording, with no alarm language
  • Points to student psychologists and 24/7 support lines in Belgium
  • Not a medical device

Tested before we scale

We back our approach with published research and we say plainly what is not proven.

  • 35 studies and reports reviewed in our evidence dossier
  • The KU Leuven pilot will test whether the app helps
  • We intend to share results openly, including unfavourable ones

What we will measure

Accountable to the pilot, not to engagement.

Our measure of success is students who feel better supported, not students who spend more time in the app. In the KU Leuven pilot we plan to look at these.

Engagement

Do students keep the check-in going, and what makes them stop?

Wellbeing

What changes over a semester, compared with a comparison group?

Help-seeking

Does the app help students reach student services, or delay it?

Safety

How often do check-ins raise stress or falsely reassure, and how do we follow up?

The pilot is still being designed with researchers and ethics advisers, and there are no results yet. Read our evidence dossier for the research behind this.
