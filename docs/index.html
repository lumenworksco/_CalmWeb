Why CalmCampus

Burnout is quiet while it builds.

It rarely arrives overnight. Sleep slips, days get smaller, deadlines stack up. Most students only reach out once it already feels heavy, and by then the way back is longer.

Support comes late

Student services mostly meet students after a rough patch has set in. We want to be there in the weeks before.

Asking for help is hard

Stigma, waiting lists and not knowing where to start keep many students from reaching out at all.

Small signs get missed

Sleep, movement and mood tell a story over time. Nobody sees it, least of all the student living in the middle of it.

The app

A calm minute, every day.

CalmCampus is a native iPhone app, with Android to follow. It listens a little, asks a little, and suggests something small that fits your day.

  1. A short daily check-in

    Three quick questions about mood, energy and stress. Every question can be skipped. When something looks off, like a short night, it may ask one gentle follow-up. Never more than five in total.

  2. Your own baseline

    Sleep and activity from your phone are compared with your own normal, not with other people. Missing data stays missing. We never fill gaps with guesses.

  3. One small step Being rebuilt

    Short breathing and grounding exercises, plus pointers to your university's student support when you want a person to talk to. We are bringing these across from our first prototype.

Privacy

Your data stays on your phone.

Privacy is how the app is built, not a line in a policy. What you share with CalmCampus is for you, and it never becomes something a university can look up.

On-device only

Check-ins and sensor data are stored on your phone. There is no server holding them.

No cloud AI

Any AI runs on the device itself. Nothing you write is sent to an outside service.

No analytics

No trackers and no ad networks. Nothing about an individual student reaches the university.

Wellness, not diagnosis

CalmCampus spots early signs and supports good habits. It does not diagnose or replace professional care.

Research

Built on research. Designed to be tested.

Small effects

Digital self-help helps students a little: g = 0.18 for depression and 0.20 for stress in 48 trials. We take that as a reason to measure carefully.

Where the evidence is thin

0.82 to 0.57

A phone-based depression model looked accurate in 57 students (AUC 0.82) and was close to chance in a mixed sample of 5,262 (AUC 0.57). That is why we compare each student with their own baseline, and ask them directly.

Müller et al., Scientific Reports, 2021

What we do not claim

  • CalmCampus is a wellness tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace student services or professional care.
  • No study has tested CalmCampus yet. The studies above tested other programmes and support the reasoning behind the design, not the product.
  • We do not claim the app can spot burnout from phone data. Sleep and activity changes are prompts to reflect, not scores or warnings.
  • Where digital self-help works, effects in students are small, and we do not claim more.

What the pilot will test

Questions only. There are no results yet, and the protocol is still being written with researchers and ethics advisers.

  • Engagement. Do students keep the daily check-in going across a semester?
  • Trust. Do students understand that their data stays on the phone, and does it matter to them?
  • Signals. Do changes from a student's own sleep and activity baseline match how they say they feel?
  • Wellbeing and help-seeking. What changes over the semester, compared with a comparison group, and does the app help or delay contact with student services?
  • Safety. How often do check-ins raise stress, or reassure someone who was not fine? How are those cases followed up?

We intend to share what we find openly, including results that do not favour us.

KU Leuven pilot

We are preparing our first pilot in Leuven.

Leuven is where CalmCampus began, at the KU Leuven KICK Challenge 2026. The pilot is the next step: put the app in real students' hands, learn what helps, and do it carefully.

What we want to learn

  • Do students keep up the daily check-in, and is a minute really light enough?
  • Which small steps do students actually try, and which ones help?
  • Does the app feel safe, kind and private to the people using it?
  • How can it sit alongside the student services that already exist?

Who we would like to talk to

  • Students who would like to try the app early and tell us honestly what works.
  • Student wellbeing teams and counsellors who can help us do this responsibly.
  • Researchers in student mental health, ethics and privacy-preserving technology.

The pilot is still being shaped and nothing is final yet. Pilot participation will be voluntary, and everything we measure will stay on-device unless a participant chooses otherwise.
