# Evidence dossier: notes on verification and downloads

Version 1.0, 9 October 2026. 35 sources, all verified. 31 are legally free to read, 4 are paywalled.

## How sources were verified

- Bibliographic details and abstracts were first read from Europe PMC records in a browser. StudentLife was verified from Crossref metadata and the author-hosted PDF, and the RIVM/Trimbos monitor from the full RIVM report.
- **Full-text check (version 1.0):** every number the dossier attributes to a source was searched for in that paper's full text, for all 29 papers in `papers/`. Every figure was found, apart from the one correction listed below. The six sources not in `papers/` were checked against their abstracts (Clement, Hertenstein, Melcher & Torous, Liu, Scott) or the author's full text read earlier (Wang).
- **Reference check (version 1.0):** authors, year, volume, issue and pages of all 34 journal and conference references were compared with Crossref. All match. Where Crossref gives an earlier online-first year (Harrer 2019, Clement 2015, Wrzus & Neubauer 2023), the dossier uses the year of the journal issue, as APA 7 recommends.
- Every number in the dossier appears in `research_log.md` next to the source it came from.

## Changes in version 1.0

- **Dutch monitor sample size corrected:** 27,446 students, as stated in the full RIVM report. Draft 1 used 27,488, taken from the English factsheet. The report also confirms 5.9% participation (of 466,313 enrolled students), 23% flourishing, and the 53% figure.
- **Author lists completed** for Auerbach 2016, Mason 2025, Ebert 2019, Freeman 2017 and Hertenstein 2019, in APA 7 format: all authors when there are 20 or fewer, otherwise the first 19, an ellipsis, then the last author. Ebert 2019's author initials were corrected from Crossref and the paper (Martínez, K. U.; Kuechler, A.-M.).
- Cover changed from "Draft 1" to "Version 1.0".

## Corrections to the brief

- **Lindsay et al. 2022** is real and the DOI is correct, but its title is "Psychological predictors of insomnia, anxiety and depression in university students: potential prevention targets". Its main focus is perfectionism, self-esteem and locus of control as predictors of insomnia. Insomnia predicting depression and anxiety is a secondary finding (β = 0.15 and 0.16). It is a Canadian sample (Queen's University), not European.
- **Auerbach et al. 2018** is in the *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* 127(7), 623–638, doi:10.1037/abn0000362. The 35% figure (lifetime) and n = 13,984 are both confirmed in the abstract. The matching 12-month figure is 31%.
- **RIVM/Trimbos 2025** is a government report, not peer reviewed: Nuijen et al., RIVM-rapport 2025-0106, doi:10.21945/RIVM-2025-0106.
- **StudentLife (Wang et al. 2014)** is a conference paper in the UbiComp '14 proceedings, pages 3–14. It had 48 students in one class at one college.

## Unverified (left out of the dossier)

- **RIVM/Trimbos 2025, chart values**: emotional exhaustion (56% in 2025), loneliness (53%), performance pressure (60%) and high stress (41%) appear in Figure 1 of the English factsheet but could not be tied to their labels with certainty, so they are not used. The full report is now in `papers/` (Table 2.1) if you want to add them.
- **RIVM/Trimbos 2025, "feelings of anxiety or depression" (83%)**: the indicator definition was not available on the page I read, so the number is not used. It probably counts any level of symptoms and would be misleading without the definition.
- **Burnout prevalence in Belgian or Dutch students specifically**: no peer-reviewed study was found and verified in this round. The burnout figures in the dossier are global and from the pandemic period (Abraham 2024).
- **Madigan & Curran 2021** (burnout and academic achievement, *Educational Psychology Review*) was a candidate source but could not be opened, so it is not included.

Paywalled, not downloaded (DOI and link are in `index.csv`): Clement et al. 2015, Hertenstein et al. 2019, Melcher & Torous 2020, Liu et al. 2026. KU Leuven library access will cover these.

The open-access types differ. Some are publisher open access (CC licences). Others are author manuscripts in PubMed Central (Bruffaerts 2018, Auerbach 2016 and 2018, Mason 2025) or free copies there (Harrer 2019), an accepted manuscript in a university repository (Schuch 2018) or an author-hosted PDF (Wang 2014). All are legal to download for private reading. Do not redistribute non-CC versions; link to them instead.
